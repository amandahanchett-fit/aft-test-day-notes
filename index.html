AFT Test Day Checklist: What to Bring and Do Before You Score

Test day is where months of training either show up or fall apart. A simple checklist removes the small mistakes that cost points. This guide is written for soldiers and recruits who want a calm, repeatable routine for the Army Fitness Test.

The night before, treat sleep as your first event. Aim for seven to nine hours, keep dinner familiar, and skip anything new. Drink water through the afternoon and evening rather than chugging it in the morning. Lay out your uniform, shoes, socks, watch and ID so the morning has no decisions in it.

On the morning of the test, eat a light breakfast two to three hours ahead if your stomach allows it. Oats, a banana, toast with peanut butter or eggs are common choices. Keep caffeine at the amount you already use in training. Arrive early enough to walk the venue, find the start lines and listen to the briefing without rushing.

A proper warm up matters more than most people think. Spend ten minutes on easy jogging, leg swings, hip openers, arm circles, bodyweight squats and a few light practice reps of each event. For the deadlift, do two or three warm up sets with lighter weight so your back and hamstrings are ready. For the plank, brace for a few short holds. For the run, finish with four short strides.

Know the order and the rest rules. The events are the three repetition maximum deadlift, hand release push up, sprint drag carry, plank and the two mile run. Ask the grader about any rule you are unsure of before you start, not after. Hand release push ups need full chest contact and a visible hand release, and the plank needs a neutral spine and steady form.

Pacing is the last big lever. Start the two mile run at a pace you could hold for a bit longer than the distance, then speed up in the final half mile. Most people lose points by starting too fast and fading. Use a watch if allowed and check each lap against your goal split.

Afterwards, walk, hydrate and write down what happened: your raw numbers, how each event felt and what you would change. Raw performance converts into points through age and gender neutral scoring tables, so your own record helps you set realistic targets for the next test. When you have your numbers, you can run them through this scoring tool to see where each event lands and which event gives you the cheapest extra points.

A final tip is mental. Pick one cue per event, such as chest up for the deadlift, hips low for the drag, and relaxed shoulders for the run. One cue is easier to hold under stress than five. Review the checklist the night before, trust the training, and let the routine carry you through the day.
